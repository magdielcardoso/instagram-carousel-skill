# Event carousel direction

Read this reference when a conference, livestream, meetup, class, race, launch event, or other gathering is the main subject and the goal is attendance, registration, or participation. Also read [audience, value, and proof](audience-value-and-proof.md) to make the invitation relevant and substantiate its claims. This is an event-promotion direction, distinct from a carousel that teaches a subject. Adapt the visual concepts to the user's brand and supplied assets; do not reproduce a reference's exact layout, artwork, or type treatment.

## Event design principles

- Establish the event's identity with a visual motif derived from its subject, venue, activity, or central idea. Reuse it across slides with changes in scale, crop, or placement so the campaign feels connected but not mechanically repeated.
- Create energy through expressive type and vector forms. Large condensed type, diagonal bands, outlines, cutouts, paths, and directional shapes can suggest movement even when there is no suitable human photo. Keep the event name and key information readable first.
- Choose a dominant brand color, a restrained accent palette, and neutrals for legibility. Give saturated fields quieter regions for type and practical details. Check contrast on the final composite.
- Make the event name or central promise the main headline. Display type may be unusually large or condensed when it fits the identity, but the whole title must remain legible in the feed thumbnail and not collide with essential details.
- Group confirmed details so they are quick to scan: date, time, location or platform, speakers, program, price, and registration route as available. Labels should accompany icons; color alone must not carry meaning.
- Use distinct but related compositions across the sequence. A typographic announcement, a reason to attend, confirmed agenda or speaker details, a logistics-focused slide, and an action-led close can create rhythm without repeating one poster layout.
- Use vectors, diagrams, maps, symbols, or approved event imagery when they clarify the experience. Do not invent speakers, venue, schedule, price, capacity, registration status, or deadlines to fill the layout.
- Make the next action explicit: register, save the date, join, or share, according to the user's goal. Keep CTA wording and destination accurate.

## Possible story arc

Use only the steps supported by confirmed information and the requested slide count.

1. Event identity, theme, or date.
2. Why this audience may want to attend.
3. Confirmed experience, speakers, or agenda.
4. Date, time, venue/platform, or practical details.
5. Registration or attendance CTA.

## Keep the objective distinct

Use this direction only when promoting the event is the main goal. If the carousel explains a concept and merely mentions an upcoming event at the end, keep the editorial structure; do not turn the whole sequence into an event campaign.
